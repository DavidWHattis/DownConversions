Cambridge downconversions

Prior to multifamily zoning reform, zoning imposed strict limits on what could be built in Cambridge neighborhoods. However, this did not mean that there was no investment in the city's housing stock, or that residential developments were not possible. Property owners tended to invest in their properties through renovations or new developments with few homes. Developments that reduced the number of homes were common. This page shows a list of these down conversion developments that have occurred in Cambridge, taken from the city's permits database. Since multifamily zoning passed in 2025, consistent with what CDD has reported, we find that the number of down conversion developments has decreased. Instead, developments proposed since 2025 are more likely to be adding homes. Exclusionary zoning never protected old rental housing; it exacerbated the shortage of housing that drove up housing prices citywide.

A downconversion is a permit that lowers a building's home count but leaves at least one home, such as a two-family house becoming a single-family house. Each record is one address.

About these numbers

  • What counts. The permit reduces a building's homes and at least one home remains. Demolitions with nothing left standing are not included.
  • Homes lost is the net change reported for each address. Homes before and after are the permit's own counts.
  • One row per address. If an address had several permits, their home counts are added together and the latest permit year is shown.
  • 2026 is a partial year. The permit file was exported on November 7, 2026, so the last column is paler.
Data: City of Cambridge housing permits database. Photos: Cambridge Assessor property database and the city’s AxisGIS historical viewer. Each address is summed across its permits; see “About these numbers” above.
